# Your phishing-triage side gig competes with Proofpoint

> **Read this first.** Your actual Proofpoint offer letter, invention-assignment agreement and current internal code of conduct are private documents. Nothing here is based on them. Everything below is inferred from public material: a 2019 public-company version of Proofpoint's Business Conduct Guidelines, executive employment exhibits Proofpoint filed with the SEC in 2011-2012, court records, and general US state law. Several primary sites (proofpoint.com, sec.gov, nysenate.gov) could not be opened directly during research, so some quotations come from search-engine excerpts of those pages and should be checked against the source. This is not legal advice. Your own signed documents control, and an employment/IP lawyer licensed in your state should review them before you act.

The plain answer is that the side gig you describe, an AI phishing-triage CLI for SMBs and MSPs that could become a hosted service or consulting business, sits in the middle of Proofpoint's business. Under the public versions of its rules, you would almost certainly need written approval before starting it, and Proofpoint would have a credible claim to own the code even if you write it on your own laptop on weekends. Proofpoint's public conduct code bars employees from competing with the company or taking opportunities it "might want to pursue" without prior approval from the Compliance Officer or Board. It also says that even independently sourced opportunities "may be questionable" if they relate to Proofpoint's lines of business ([Business Conduct Guidelines](https://investors.proofpoint.com/investors/governance/governance-overview/business-conduct-guidelines/default.aspx)). Proofpoint already sells an MSP-focused Microsoft 365 suite with automatic post-delivery remediation, a phishing-report button, closed-loop triage automation and a managed abuse-mailbox service ([Proofpoint MSP page](https://proofpoint.com/us/partners/msp); [Managed Abuse Mailbox](https://www.proofpoint.com/uk/products/premium-services/managed-abuse-mailbox)). Your role as a TAP/TRAP/API support SME gives you exactly the customer and product knowledge that trade-secret claims are built on. Proofpoint has also taken a former email-security employee to a jury verdict for breaching his inventions agreement ([The Register](https://theregister.com/2021/08/24/proofpoint_wins_14m_vade/)). The paid hosted version is the riskiest form of this idea and is realistically incompatible with staying employed there. A free open-source CLI is lower risk but still needs written sign-off. The safest moves are to get your documents, disclose before you write code, or pick a niche that is not email security.

## Proofpoint's public code bans competing and requires advance approval

The most recent public employee code found is Proofpoint's "Business Conduct Guidelines," which apply to "all employees, officers and directors" worldwide. The indexed copy was last modified in July 2019, before Thoma Bravo took the company private in 2021 ([Business Conduct Guidelines](https://investors.proofpoint.com/investors/governance/governance-overview/business-conduct-guidelines/default.aspx); [SEC PREM14A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1212458/000114036121019547/nt10024151x1_prem14a.htm)). Proofpoint's 2025 ESG policy and its modern slavery statement still refer to an ethics "Code" and to "Business Conduct Guidelines," so the framework appears to continue. The current internal text is not public ([ESG Policy 2025](https://www.proofpoint.com/sites/default/files/legal-documents/pfpt-en-proofpoint-esg-policy-approved-7.23.25.pdf); [AU Modern Slavery statement](https://www.proofpoint.com/sites/default/files/legal-documents/pfpt-en-au-modern-slavery-act-and-human-trafficking-statement.pdf)).

Four provisions in the 2019 text bear on your plan. First, a conflict exists when a private interest "interferes in any way — or even appears to interfere" with Proofpoint's interests, and anyone aware of a potential conflict must raise it "immediately" with a manager, HR or Legal. Second, **employees "may not compete with Proofpoint or take personal advantage of business opportunities that Proofpoint might want to pursue" without prior approval from the Compliance Officer or the Board.** Third, employees may not work for, consult for or "receive compensation for personal services from" any Proofpoint customer, partner, reseller, distributor or competitor. That matters because many MSPs are Proofpoint partners or resellers. Fourth, outside roles at unrelated companies require advance approval from your manager, HR and the General Counsel ([Business Conduct Guidelines](https://investors.proofpoint.com/investors/governance/governance-overview/business-conduct-guidelines/default.aspx)).

The Guidelines also say that **even "opportunities that are acquired through independent sources may be questionable if they are related to Proofpoint's existing or proposed lines of business."** Doing the work on your own time and equipment therefore does not get you outside the clause. The text says nothing explicit about unpaid open-source work. A free CLI is not "compensation from" anyone, but it can still be a "potential conflict" or a form of "compet[ing]," and you would have a duty to raise it. Confidentiality runs "during your employment with Proofpoint and thereafter" and covers "technology and technical information, product designs, roadmaps, and business processes." Posting proprietary information on LinkedIn or web forums needs express authorization ([Business Conduct Guidelines](https://investors.proofpoint.com/investors/governance/governance-overview/business-conduct-guidelines/default.aspx)). No public Proofpoint policy on employee open-source contributions, GitHub use or conference talks turned up.

## The inventions agreement probably assigns this exact project to Proofpoint

Proofpoint's SEC filings describe a standard three-part employment package: an offer letter, a **Proprietary Information and Inventions Agreement (PIIA)** and an Arbitration Agreement. Together these "form the complete and exclusive statement" of employment terms ([SEC S-1 exhibits](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001212458/000104746911010082/a2206553zex-10_10.htm)). The filed PIIA uses a present-tense assignment ("I hereby assign to the Company any Rights I may have or acquire in Proprietary Information"). It includes a prior-inventions schedule and excludes only inventions protected under California Labor Code §2870 ([Fintel copy of EX-10.10](https://fintel.io/doc/sec-pfpt-proofpoint-ex1010-2012-january-25-18594-17)). These are executive forms from 2009-2012. A rank-and-file support agreement from the Thoma Bravo era may be worded differently, but the structure is a reasonable baseline.

The weakness for you is that §2870 and its copies protect inventions made entirely on your own time without employer equipment or trade secrets, **except inventions that "relate … to the employer's business, or actual or demonstrably anticipated research or development"** or that result from work you did for the employer ([Cal. Labor Code §2870](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=2870)). An AI phishing-triage tool built by a Proofpoint threat-response support engineer relates to Proofpoint's business by any reading. The personal-time safe harbor is therefore unlikely to help, whatever state you are in.

Your state matters less than you might hope. On the East Coast, New York (Labor Law §203-f, since September 2023), New Jersey (N.J.S.A. 34:1B-265), Delaware (19 Del. C. §805) and North Carolina (G.S. 66-57.1) have §2870-style statutes. **All of them keep the same "relates to the employer's business" exception** ([Cozen O'Connor on NY](https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/publications/2023/new-law-in-ny-prohibits-certain-employee-invention-assignment-agreements); [Justia NJ](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-34/section-34-1b-265/); [Justia DE](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/title-19/chapter-8/section-805); [NC G.S. 66-57.1](https://ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_66/GS_66-57.1.html)). New Jersey's version leaves out the word "directly," which arguably widens the employer's exception ([Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2018/07/invention-assignment-agreements-how-to-avoid-pitf/)). Florida, Georgia, Pennsylvania, Massachusetts, Virginia, Maryland, Connecticut and the other East Coast states have no such statute, so a broad assignment clause there is generally enforced as written. North Carolina explicitly puts the burden of proof on the employee, and a California federal court did the same under §2870 ([NC G.S. 66-57.1](https://ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_66/GS_66-57.1.html); [Venture Corp. v. Barrett](https://docs.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/5:2013cv03384/268370/278)). New York's off-duty conduct law does not help either. It protects only uncompensated recreational activity and excludes anything that "creates a material conflict of interest" with the employer's proprietary or business interests ([NY Labor Law 201-d](https://nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LAB/201-D)).

Putting an open-source license on the code does not fix any of this. If Proofpoint owns the code, you had no right to license it, and a permissive or AGPL license is invalid ([HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21786111)). The other side matters too: courts do read assignment wording closely. In *Mattel v. MGA* the Ninth Circuit found "at any time during my employment" ambiguous ([Ninth Circuit opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2010/07/28/09-55673.pdf)). Your exact clause can still help or hurt you, which is why reading it comes first.

## Proofpoint already sells this product to your target buyers

The commercial overlap is direct, not adjacent. Proofpoint says Essentials "is now Proofpoint 365 Total Protection: an all-in-one Microsoft 365 security suite built for MSPs." It comes with tiered MSP partner levels, monthly utility billing and a multi-tenant console ([Proofpoint MSP page](https://proofpoint.com/us/partners/msp); [MSP solution brief](https://www.proofpoint.com/sites/default/files/solution-briefs/pfpt-us-sb-msp-essentials.pdf)). Essentials release notes describe automatic post-delivery retraction, which gained TOAD and impostor classifications as remediation triggers in August 2025 ([Essentials Release Notes](https://help.proofpoint.com/Essentials/Additional_Resources/Release_Notes)). Higher up the stack, PhishAlarm, CLEAR and TRAP automate the path from a user's report through analysis to quarantine, and Managed Abuse Mailbox sells human-assisted triage of reported mail ([CLEAR](https://www.proofpoint.com/us/corporate-blog/post/new-clear-solution-automation-email-reporting-through-remediation); [TRAP](https://www.proofpoint.com/au/products/email-protection/threat-response-auto-pull); [Managed Abuse Mailbox](https://www.proofpoint.com/uk/products/premium-services/managed-abuse-mailbox)).

An "AI phishing-triage service for MSPs" is close to a description of that product line. It also falls within any reasonable reading of "demonstrably anticipated research or development," since the whole vendor market is moving toward AI triage. Proofpoint's own model makes the corporate-opportunity argument stronger. It runs a free community ruleset (ET Open) next to a paid tier built on "internal Proofpoint intelligence, threat hunting, and detection" ([Proofpoint ET blog](https://www.proofpoint.com/us/blog/threat-insight/emerging-threats-updates-improve-metadata-including-mitre-attck-tags)). A free open-source CLI that funnels users to a paid hosted tier follows the same playbook, aimed at the same customers.

Your role adds a further problem. A TAP/TRAP support SME knows which MSPs struggle with false positives or abuse-mailbox volume, how TAP classifies threats, how TRAP matches and pulls messages, which lures evade detection, and what customers have asked for in tickets. Building a tool around those gaps fits the "relates to the business" and "results from work performed for the employer" exceptions most clearly. It also invites a trade-secret claim even if you never copy a file. Customer-submitted phishing samples are customer data and should never touch the project.

## Proofpoint has sued a former employee over email-security know-how and won

In *Proofpoint/Cloudmark v. Vade Secure & Olivier Lemarié* (N.D. Cal. No. 3:19-cv-04238), Proofpoint alleged that Lemarié, a former Cloudmark engineer and executive, breached his "Employee Proprietary Information and Inventions Agreement" by using Cloudmark's proprietary information to build Vade's Office 365 integration ([vLex order](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/proofpoint-inc-v-vade-887832893)). In August 2021 a jury found that Vade willfully and maliciously misappropriated 15 of 20 trade secrets and infringed copyright. It also found that **Lemarié personally breached his agreement**, awarding about $13.4M in unjust enrichment plus $480K for breach of contract ([The Register](https://theregister.com/2021/08/24/proofpoint_wins_14m_vade/); [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/vade-secure-ordered-pay-14-million-proofpoint-ip-theft-lawsuit)). The Ninth Circuit affirmed in August 2024 ([Law360](https://www.law360.com/articles/1875252)). The case was not all one-sided. The court rejected one breach theory and denied a permanent injunction against Lemarié because there was no evidence he still held the information ([Justia doc. 820](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/3:2019cv04238/345242/820)). Courts do limit overreach, but that did not spare him years of federal litigation.

In 2021 Proofpoint also sued former channel sales director Samuel Boone, one of seven employees who moved to Abnormal Security. Boone admitted leaving with a USB drive of work documents ([CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/3631491/proofpoint-lawsuits-underscore-risk-of-employee-offboarding.html)). No Proofpoint non-compete suit was found, which is consistent with a company that relies on trade-secret, PIIA and copyright claims instead. The pattern across the industry is the same. The Texas court in *Alcatel v. Brown* held that an employee's undocumented idea for solving a work problem was an invention he had to disclose, after he asked for a release and was fired ([Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/ex-employee8217s-idea-is-owned-by-alcatel)). In *Levandowski*, copying files to a personal laptop ended in an 18-month prison sentence ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ndca/pr/former-uber-executive-sentenced-18-months-jail-trade-secret-theft-google)). The research found no case of a Proofpoint employee being sued over a free personal tool. The risk is real but not documented for that exact scenario.

Non-competes are a separate, mostly post-employment question. The FTC's nationwide ban is dead: the agency voted in September 2025 to drop its appeals ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/federal-trade-commission-files-accede-vacatur-non-compete-clause-rule)). East Coast rules vary widely, from Florida's CHOICE Act, which allows up to four-year covenants for high earners, to strict limits in DC, Massachusetts and Virginia ([Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/5/florida-choice-act-new-protections-for-noncompete-and-garden-leave-agreements); [DC DOES](https://does.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/does/publication/attachments/2026%20Ban%20on%20Non-Compete%20Clauses.pdf); [M.G.L. c.149 §24L](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section24l)). While you still work there, the conduct code and the duty of loyalty are what constrain you, and they already rule out competing.

## Risk by version of the idea

| Version | Risk | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Paid hosted triage SaaS for SMBs/MSPs | **Very high**; realistically requires leaving Proofpoint | Direct competition with 365 Total Protection, CLEAR and Managed Abuse Mailbox in the same channel; corporate-opportunity clause; likely IP assignment; Vade-style trade-secret profile |
| Paid email-security triage consulting for MSPs | **High** | Paid outside work for possible Proofpoint partners or customers is barred; competes for services revenue; risk of using customer knowledge |
| Free open-source CLI for M365/Google Workspace | **Medium-high**; viable only with written approval | No revenue conflict, but still "relates to the business," so Proofpoint can claim ownership; could look like a free substitute for paid features |
| Private portfolio project, never published | **Medium** | Same ownership problem, less exposure, little public value |
| Content and teaching from public examples | **Lower** | Less exposed to invention assignment; confidentiality and social-media rules still apply |
| Non-email or non-security tooling | **Lowest** | Falls outside "relates to the business" if it is truly unrelated |

## Action checklist

| # | Action | Detail |

| 1 | **Stop writing project code for now** | Every commit made before you get clearance adds to Proofpoint's ownership claim and to the record against you |

| 6 | **Expect a no on the paid versions** | If you are refused, take it in writing, comply, and do not quietly proceed. If commercializing matters to you more than the job, the cleaner path is to leave first, honor confidentiality and non-solicit terms, and build from scratch |
| 7 | **If approved, keep strict separation** | Use a personal laptop never enrolled in company MDM; personal GitHub, email, cloud and LLM accounts paid by you; no company VPN or Wi-Fi; no work during shifts or on-call. Commit timestamps are evidence |

Use public corpora and synthetic mail only. | 10 | **Stay away from Proofpoint commercially** | Do not integrate with or replicate Proofpoint products, benchmark against them, market to Proofpoint customers or reseller MSPs, mention your title, or bring the tool up on support calls |

## Lower-risk niches that still use your skills

Email-adjacent work is never fully safe at Proofpoint, because its portfolio covers email, awareness training, DLP and archiving. Each option below still needs a check against your agreement's "relates to the business" wording. In rough order from safest to least safe:

| Niche | Why it's safer | Remaining risk |
|---|---|---|
| Non-security support-engineering tooling, such as an LLM ticket-triage or knowledge-base CLI | Uses your support and API skills outside Proofpoint's market | Low; avoid security framing |
| Linux, CLI or LLM-ops tooling unrelated to email | Clearly outside the business | Low |
| Contributing to existing neutral open-source projects, such as header parsers or MIME libraries | Not a branded product that competes with Proofpoint | Low-medium; code related to the business can still be claimed |
| SOC/IR training labs, CTF content, or writing and teaching on header forensics and LLM prompting for analysts, all built from public examples | Content is less exposed to invention assignment | Medium; publishing and confidentiality rules apply, so check for speaking pre-approval |
| DMARC/SPF tooling or deliverability help for nonprofits and small organizations | Email authentication is often not a Proofpoint product line | Medium; verify against the portfolio first |
| Security-awareness content for a vertical such as K-12 or churches | Small market, low revenue | Medium-high; Proofpoint sells awareness training |

## Conclusion

The core problem is not side gigs in general. It is that this particular idea is the one Proofpoint is most likely to see as its own. Your expertise is what makes the product credible, and that same expertise is what gives Proofpoint a claim to it. Separation habits such as weekends only and a personal laptop are necessary, but they do not change who owns the code when the "relates to the business" exception applies. The approval in writing is what actually protects you. Ask for it before you build anything, and ask for the full scope at once, because the history in this space shows that asking after the work already exists is what gets employees fired and sued. If the answer on a paid product is no, the real choice is between this job and this business, and the business is best started after you leave, from scratch.
