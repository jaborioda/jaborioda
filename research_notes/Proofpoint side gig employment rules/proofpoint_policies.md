## 1. Code of conduct: outside employment, conflicts of interest, corporate opportunities, competing, disclosure/approval

### Takeaway
The most recent public Proofpoint employee code found is the "Business Conduct Guidelines" on the legacy investor-relations site; the indexed copy was last modified July 24, 2019. It (a) bans any employee work or paid services for a Proofpoint competitor, customer, partner, supplier, reseller, licensee or affiliate; (b) requires advance approval from the employee's manager, HR and the General Counsel for outside roles at unrelated companies; and (c) bars competing with Proofpoint or taking opportunities Proofpoint "might want to pursue" without prior approval from the Compliance Officer or Board. It also says that even opportunities from independent sources "may be questionable" if they relate to Proofpoint's existing or proposed lines of business. An AI phishing-triage tool for SMBs/MSPs falls squarely within Proofpoint's lines of business.

### Cited Findings
- Document identity and scope: Proofpoint's employee code is titled "Business Conduct Guidelines" and is hosted at investors.proofpoint.com. It "appl[ies] to all employees, officers and directors and to Proofpoint affiliates and subsidiaries worldwide." Proofpoint "expects everyone to act with the highest standards of honesty and ethical conduct." — [Proofpoint Investors – Business Conduct Guidelines](https://investors.proofpoint.com/investors/governance/governance-overview/business-conduct-guidelines/default.aspx)
- Version/date: the search index reported the indexed page as last modified July 24, 2019. No later version date could be confirmed. — [Proofpoint Investors – Business Conduct Guidelines](https://investors.proofpoint.com/investors/governance/governance-overview/business-conduct-guidelines/default.aspx)
- Conflict definition: "A 'conflict of interest' occurs when a person's private interest (or the interest of a member of his or her family) interferes in any way - or even appears to interfere - with the interests of Proofpoint as a whole." — [Business Conduct Guidelines](https://investors.proofpoint.com/investors/governance/governance-overview/business-conduct-guidelines/default.aspx)
- Disclosure duty: "Anyone who becomes aware of a conflict or a potential conflict should bring it to the attention of a supervisor or manager, Human Resources or the Legal Department immediately." — [Business Conduct Guidelines](https://investors.proofpoint.com/investors/governance/governance-overview/business-conduct-guidelines/default.aspx)

### Inferences
- Under the 2019 text, a commercial hosted phishing-triage SaaS for SMBs/MSPs would very likely count as "compet[ing] with Proofpoint" or as an opportunity Proofpoint "might want to pursue." Proofpoint sells email protection and threat response to SMBs through MSP channels; Essentials is a product line verified elsewhere, not here. The Guidelines require prior approval from the Compliance Officer or Board for that. The "independent sources" sentence means building it entirely on personal time does not by itself take it outside the clause.
- A free, non-commercial open-source CLI is a closer call. It is not "work for or compensation from" a third party, but it could still be treated as "compet[ing]" or as a "potential conflict" that must be raised "immediately" with a manager, HR or Legal. The text does not expressly address unpaid open-source work.
- Selling to MSPs that are Proofpoint resellers/partners, or to Proofpoint customers, would also hit the "customer, partner ... reseller" prohibition on receiving compensation.
- The approval chain the text names (manager, HR and General Counsel for outside roles; Compliance Officer or Board for competing opportunities) suggests a written disclosure/approval request is the expected route. These Guidelines are a 2019 public-company document. The post-2021 private-company internal version may differ.

## 2. Confidential information, trade secrets, threat intel, customer data, company equipment/time

### Takeaway
The Guidelines impose a confidentiality duty that lasts during employment and after it. The duty covers technology, product designs, roadmaps and business processes. Employees need express authorization before posting proprietary or confidential information on social media or web forums, and may not discuss company business in online forums even under a pseudonym. No public text specifically covering threat-intelligence data, customer email data, or use of company equipment/time was retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- "Non-public Proofpoint information is a vital company asset. You have a duty to maintain the confidentiality of Proofpoint information both during your employment with Proofpoint and thereafter." — [Business Conduct Guidelines](https://investors.proofpoint.com/investors/governance/governance-overview/business-conduct-guidelines/default.aspx)
- Covered information includes "financial analyses planning, forecasts and budgets, business and marketing plans, technology and technical information, product designs, roadmaps, and business processes." — [Business Conduct Guidelines](https://investors.proofpoint.com/investors/governance/governance-overview/business-conduct-guidelines/default.aspx)
- Social media: "express authorization should be obtained before posting any proprietary or confidential Proofpoint information on forums such as Facebook, Twitter, LinkedIn, or other social media, or other web-based forums." Per the search summary, employees also may not discuss company business in online forums, whether under their own name or a pseudonym. Employees are told not to discuss sensitive matters in public places. — [Business Conduct Guidelines](https://investors.proofpoint.com/investors/governance/governance-overview/business-conduct-guidelines/default.aspx)
- Material non-public information falls under a separate insider-trading policy. This mattered while Proofpoint was public; it was taken private by Thoma Bravo in 2021, per the merger proxy. — [Business Conduct Guidelines](https://investors.proofpoint.com/investors/governance/governance-overview/business-conduct-guidelines/default.aspx); [Proofpoint PREM14A (2021) on SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1212458/000114036121019547/nt10024151x1_prem14a.htm)
- Proofpoint's ET Pro ruleset "contains additional rules based on internal Proofpoint intelligence, threat hunting, and detection," as distinct from the free community ET Open ruleset. This shows Proofpoint treats its internal threat intelligence as a commercial asset. — [Proofpoint ET Intelligence product page](https://www.proofpoint.com/us/blog/threat-insight/emerging-threats-updates-improve-metadata-including-mitre-attck-tags); [eWeek 2015 on ET acquisition](https://www.eweek.com/?p=30848)

### Inferences
- For a TAP/TRAP/API support SME, the riskiest inputs to a side project are: customer threat data seen in support cases, internal detection logic and heuristics, TAP/TRAP API behaviors learned from internal docs, product roadmaps, and support playbooks. All of these plausibly fall within "technology and technical information ... product designs, roadmaps, and business processes."
- Public Proofpoint API documentation is public, so a tool that integrates with TAP/TRAP via documented APIs is not inherently confidential. Using undocumented internals or support-only knowledge is where risk lies (inference).
- Promoting the project on LinkedIn or forums in ways that discuss Proofpoint business would be subject to the "express authorization" rule.
- The Vade case (section 4) shows that Proofpoint treats code and design knowledge carried into a new venture as trade-secret misappropriation even when no files were bulk-copied in an obvious way.

### Gaps
- No public Proofpoint text found on acceptable use of company laptops/accounts/time, customer data handling by employees, or AI-tool use. An internal acceptable-use or information-security policy almost certainly exists but is not public.

## 4. Litigation history: former employees, trade secrets, non-compete/non-solicit

### Takeaway
Proofpoint actively litigates against former employees and the competitors who hire them. In *Proofpoint/Cloudmark v. Vade Secure & Olivier Lemarié* (N.D. Cal. No. 3:19-cv-04238-MMC), a jury in August 2021 found that Vade willfully and maliciously misappropriated 15 of 20 trade secrets and infringed copyright. It also found that Lemarié, a former Cloudmark engineer and executive, breached his Employee Proprietary Information and Inventions Agreement (PIIA). Damages were about $13.5M, and the Ninth Circuit affirmed in August 2024. In 2021 Proofpoint also sued former channel sales director Samuel Boone, who had joined Abnormal Security. No non-compete suit was found, which is consistent with California law.

### Cited Findings
- Case: *Proofpoint, Inc. et al v. Vade Secure, Inc. et al*, No. 3:19-cv-04238-MMC (N.D. Cal., Judge Maxine Chesney), filed 2019. — [Justia docket doc. 820](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/3:2019cv04238/345242/820); [Law360](https://www.law360.com/articles/1415180/proofpoint-entitled-to-14m-in-trade-secrets-case-jury-says)
- The contract count: plaintiffs alleged Lemarié violated "his employment agreement, titled 'Employee Proprietary Information and Inventions Agreement' ('PIIA')." The core allegation was that he used Cloudmark's proprietary information in developing Vade's Microsoft Office 365 integration. — [vLex, order of Jun 4, 2021](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/proofpoint-inc-v-vade-887832893); [Casemine](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/60c872c34653d035f7b0c41b)
- Verdict (Aug 2021): the jury found Vade misappropriated 15 of 20 trade secrets willfully and maliciously, infringed copyright, and found that Lemarié breached his employment agreement. Damages were $13.4M for unjust enrichment, $480K for breach of contract, and $0 for actual loss. — [The Register, 2021-08-24](https://theregister.com/2021/08/24/proofpoint_wins_14m_vade/); [Proofpoint press release](https://www.proofpoint.com/us/newsroom/press-releases/us-federal-jury-unanimously-finds-vade-secure-willfully-misappropriated); [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/vade-secure-ordered-pay-14-million-proofpoint-ip-theft-lawsuit); [HG.org](https://www.hg.org/legal-articles/theft-of-trade-secrets-results-in-14m-judgment-60109)
- Per the court's order, Vade misappropriated Trade Secrets 1-7 and 9-16. Lemarié misappropriated 1-7 and 9-15 but not willfully and maliciously. — [Justia doc. 820](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/3:2019cv04238/345242/820)
- Damages figures differ slightly by outlet: Channel Futures reports "$13.5 million in compensatory damages". — [Channel Futures](https://channelfutures.com/regulation-compliance/jury-vade-secure-willfully-and-maliciously-misappropriated-proofpoint-trade-secrets)
- Post-trial: exemplary damages were denied on Nov 18, 2021, and attorney fees were denied in 2023. The court denied a permanent injunction against Lemarié, noting there was no evidence he still held the information, and granted one against Vade. — [Justia doc. 820](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/3:2019cv04238/345242/820); [Law360 on fee denial](https://law360.com/employment-authority/articles/1718432); [Justia doc. 860 (2022)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/3:2019cv04238/345242/860/)
- At summary judgment, the court ruled for Lemarié on one breach theory concerning the "DNS Security Notebook". — [vLex](https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/proofpoint-inc-v-vade-887832893)
- Appeal: the Ninth Circuit (Nos. 23-16085, 23-16175; Graber, Callahan, Koh) AFFIRMED in an unpublished memorandum on Aug 30, 2024. It declined to double the $13.5M award via exemplary damages. Vade reportedly paid the full judgment of $13,495,659.00 plus interest. — [Law360, "9th Circ. Won't Double..."](https://www.law360.com/articles/1875252); [CounselStack opinion](https://law.counselstack.com/opinion/proofpoint-inc-v-vade-usa-inc-ca9-2024)
- Vade's response at the time: "pleased that the jury saw that Proofpoint/Cloudmark's claims were an overreach." — [The Register](https://theregister.com/2021/08/24/proofpoint_wins_14m_vade/)
- Proofpoint v. Boone (Abnormal Security), 2021: court documents show Proofpoint was "piqued that seven of its employees moved on to Abnormal Security." Proofpoint alleged Abnormal targeted them for confidential information. Former channel sales director Samuel Boone admitted leaving with a "USB drive containing some of his work-related documents from Proofpoint." Boone disputed the claims. His counsel said Proofpoint sought to coerce Abnormal into not hiring its approximately 3,800 at-will employees. — [CSO Online, Christopher Burgess (2021)](https://www.csoonline.com/article/3631491/proofpoint-lawsuits-underscore-risk-of-employee-offboarding.html)
- No Proofpoint lawsuit to enforce a non-compete was found in searches. — searches cited above (negative result)

### Inferences
- Lessons for the user:
  1. Proofpoint treats its PIIA as an enforceable contract, and a jury awarded contract damages against an individual engineer, not just his new employer.
  2. Proofpoint claims trade secrets in email-security code and design, which is the exact domain of the user's project.
  3. Proofpoint sues both individuals and the companies that hire them.
  4. Taking work documents on a USB or personal device, or reusing support/engineering knowledge in a competing product, is the fact pattern Proofpoint has litigated.
  5. Proofpoint watches competitors in its space (Abnormal, Vade), and a hosted SaaS in phishing triage would fall in that space.
- California Business & Professions Code §16600 generally voids post-employment non-competes. This is general legal knowledge, not sourced here. Proofpoint, headquartered in Sunnyvale, CA, appears to rely on trade-secret, PIIA and copyright claims instead. A US East Coast employee may be subject to a different state's law and choice-of-law clause (inference; check the user's own agreement).
- The DNS Security Notebook ruling and the denial of an injunction against Lemarié show that courts do limit overreaching claims. They do not lower the cost of being sued.

- Boone case: court, docket number and outcome were not found.
- No Proofpoint non-solicitation lawsuit details were found.

## 5. SEC filings: form employment agreements, offer letters, PIIA clauses

### Takeaway
Proofpoint's 2011 S-1 and 2012 S-1/A (CIK 1212458) attach executive offer letters and an "Employee Proprietary Information and Inventions Agreement" (filed as EX-10.10 on Jan 25, 2012). The filings show a standard three-document structure: offer letter, PIIA and Arbitration Agreement. The PIIA contains a present assignment ("I hereby assign") of Proprietary Information rights, a carve-out for inventions protected by California Labor Code §2870, a prior-inventions schedule, and coverage of work done before signing.

### Cited Findings

- PIIA, EX-10.10 to S-1/A filed Jan 25, 2012: titled "Employee Proprietary Information and Inventions Agreement" and attached to an EVP Engineering offer letter; the signature date shown is 10/22/09. Assignment: "I hereby assign to the Company any Rights I may have or acquire in Proprietary Information." The assignment excludes "those Inventions, if any, the assignment of which is prohibited under California Labor Code Section 2870." The section heading is "Proprietary Information: Definition and Ownership." The obligations and definitions apply equally to related work performed before execution. A prior-inventions schedule is included; the filed copy marks "No Inventions." — [Fintel copy of EX-10.10 (Jan 25, 2012)](https://fintel.io/doc/sec-pfpt-proofpoint-ex1010-2012-january-25-18594-17)
- A later executive offer letter (Tracey Newell, 2013/2014 10-K exhibit) also exists. — [Justia Contracts – Newell offer letter](https://contracts.justia.com/companies/proofpoint-inc-2456/contract/402545/); [SEC 10-K 2013 Ex 10.11](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1212458/000121245814000004/proofpoint-12312013xex1011.htm)
- Background on Cal. Labor Code §2870 (from other companies' forms, not Proofpoint's): assignment provisions do not apply to an invention "developed entirely on [the employee's] own time without using the employer's equipment, supplies, facilities, or trade secret information." Exceptions apply where the invention relates to the employer's business or actual or demonstrably anticipated R&D, or results from work for the employer. — [Example form PIIA on SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1131543/000119312510250216/dex1015.htm); [Stimmel Law on employee inventions at home](https://stimmel-law.com/articles/who-owns-employees-inventions-made-home/)

### Inferences
- If the user's current agreement resembles the 2009-2012 PIIA form, the §2870 carve-out is the key issue. §2870's own exceptions remove protection for inventions that "relate ... to the employer's business, or actual or demonstrably anticipated research or development." An AI phishing-triage tool relates directly to Proofpoint's business (TAP/TRAP/Threat Response), so even a fully off-hours, personal-equipment project may be assignable to Proofpoint under such a clause. This is the single largest IP risk.
- The East Coast angle: many non-California states have analogous statutes (e.g., WA, IL, MN, NC, DE, NJ, NY), and some have none. Which statute applies depends on the user's state and the agreement's choice-of-law clause (general knowledge; not sourced here).
- The arbitration agreement in the standard package suggests employment disputes go to arbitration, but trade-secret claims with injunctive relief often carve out court access (inference; not verified for Proofpoint).
- Post-2021 agreements may have been updated by Thoma Bravo-era HR. The user's own signed PIIA/offer letter controls and should be reviewed first.
