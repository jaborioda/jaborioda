# US Tech Employment Agreements (PIIA, Confidentiality, Moonlighting, Non-Compete, Non-Solicit) and East Coast State Limits — Side-Project Lens

Context: US Eastern-time employee of an email-security vendor building an open-source AI phishing-triage tool (SMB/MSP market), possibly with a paid hosted version. State unknown. Research date: 2026-11-22. NOT legal advice. Note: many primary sites (nysenate.gov, sec.gov, fisherphillips.com, seyfarth.com, herrick.com) could not be fetched from this research environment; several findings rest on search-result summaries of law-firm alerts and statutes, flagged where relevant.

## 1. Typical PIIA clause structure, and why a side project in the employer's own domain is the riskiest case

### Takeaway
Standard tech PIIAs (often called CIIAAs/PIIAs) contain: a broad invention-assignment clause, a prior-inventions exhibit, confidentiality, outside-activity/conflicting-employment restrictions, return of property, non-solicit, and (state-dependent) a non-compete. The assignment carve-out statutes protect own-time/own-equipment inventions *only if they do not relate to the employer's business or actual/demonstrably anticipated R&D*. A phishing-triage tool built by an email-security vendor's employee almost certainly "relates to the business", so the statutory carve-outs likely do NOT protect it; ownership then turns on the contract wording and any written release/waiver obtained from the employer.

### Cited Findings
- Cooley GO publishes a standard "Employee Confidential Information and Inventions Assignment Agreement" (CIIAA) form, generated via Cooley GO Docs; these agreements are commonly called CIIAAs/PIIAs — [Cooley GO glossary](https://www.cooleygo.com/glossary/inventions-assignment-agreement/); [Cooley GO, Protecting employee-generated IP](https://www.cooleygo.com/protecting-employee-generated-intellectual-property/)
- Typical prior-inventions mechanism: employee attaches Exhibit A listing "with particularity all Inventions ... made by me prior to the commencement of the Relationship"; if nothing is listed, the employee represents there are none; employee agrees not to incorporate Prior Inventions into company work without written consent (example: Credo Semiconductor PIIA filed on SEC EDGAR) — [SEC EDGAR exhibit (via search summary)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807794/000162827921000596/filename3.htm); [LA Incubator sample PIIA](https://laincubator.org/wp-content/uploads/5-Sample-Proprietary-Information-and-Inventions-Agreement.pdf)
- The statutory carve-out model (CA Labor Code 2870, copied in NY/NJ/NC/etc.) excludes from assignment inventions developed entirely on own time without employer equipment, supplies, facilities, or trade secret information, EXCEPT inventions that (1) relate at the time of conception or reduction to practice to the employer's business or actual or demonstrably anticipated R&D, or (2) result from work performed for the employer — [Cal. Labor Code 2870 (leginfo)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=2870); same structure in [NC G.S. 66-57.1](https://ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_66/GS_66-57.1.html) and [NY Labor Law 203-f summary, Cozen O'Connor](https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/publications/2023/new-law-in-ny-prohibits-certain-employee-invention-assignment-agreements)
- NY's 203-f permits assignment of inventions that "relate at the time of conception or reduction to practice of the invention to the employer's business, or actual or demonstrably anticipated research or development" — quoted in firm alerts — [Wilson Sonsini](https://wsgr.com/en/insights/new-york-redefines-the-permissible-scope-of-invention-assignment-provisions.html); [Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/new-york-narrows-the-scope-of-employee-invention-assignment-provisions/)
- Washington and Minnesota statutes use "directly" (relates *directly* to the employer's business); New Jersey's version omits "directly", making NJ's employer exception arguably broader — [Foley, Invention assignment agreements: how to avoid pitfalls](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2018/07/invention-assignment-agreements-how-to-avoid-pitf/)
- Massachusetts' noncompete statute expressly does not cover (i.e., leaves intact) nonsolicitation, confidentiality and invention-assignment covenants — those are governed by common law; see MA statute text — [M.G.L. c.149 §24L](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section24l)

### Inferences
- "Relates to the business" is the decisive clause. For an email-security vendor (phishing detection, triage, threat intel, SOC/MSP channels), an AI phishing-triage tool for SMBs/MSPs is squarely in the product domain and plausibly in "demonstrably anticipated R&D" (vendors widely market AI triage/auto-remediation). That exception applies even in the protective states, so the employee should assume the employer has a colorable ownership claim under a standard PIIA regardless of personal time/equipment.
- Many PIIAs go further than the statute: they assign inventions "related to the Company's business" conceived "during the period of employment" whether or not on company time, and add a "present assignment" ("hereby assign") plus a duty to disclose all inventions. Some also include a post-employment holdover/presumption clause (inventions disclosed within ~6-12 months after leaving presumed company-owned). Exact wording must be checked in the user's actual agreement — this is inference from typical templates, not verified for this employer.
- Open-source release does not cure ownership: if the employer owns the code, the employee lacks authority to license it under an OSS license. Commercial hosting adds competitive/conflict-of-interest exposure beyond ownership.
- Practical mitigations commonly used: listing pre-existing work on the prior-inventions exhibit (only if it pre-dates hiring), and obtaining a written, specific carve-out/release or outside-activity approval from the employer (legal/HR) before building or publishing.

- Could not fetch the full text of Cooley/Orrick/YC templates; outside-activity, non-solicit and holdover clause wording is described from general knowledge of templates, not quoted.
- The specific employer's agreement is unknown; no public copy was located/verified.

## 2. Invention-assignment carve-out statutes: which states (esp. East Coast) have them, and exact conditions

### Takeaway
States with 2870-style statutes include CA, DE, IL, KS, MN, NV (different, pro-employer), NJ, NY, NC, UT, WA. East Coast states WITH a statute: New York (203-f, Sept. 15, 2023), New Jersey (34:1B-265, eff. 2018), Delaware (19 Del. C. §805), North Carolina (66-57.1). East Coast states WITHOUT a general invention-assignment statute (common law/contract governs): FL, GA, PA, MA, VA, MD, CT, SC, and others. No new state invention-assignment statute was found enacted in 2025-2026.

### Cited Findings
- **New York, Labor Law §203-f**: signed by Gov. Hochul Sept. 15, 2023 (S5640), effective immediately; makes unenforceable any provision requiring assignment of inventions developed entirely on the employee's own time without employer equipment, supplies, facilities, or trade secret information, except those relating to employer's business/actual or demonstrably anticipated R&D or resulting from work for the employer; applies to existing agreements to the extent they over-reach; no express private right of action (DOL enforcement per one alert) — [Cozen O'Connor](https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/publications/2023/new-law-in-ny-prohibits-certain-employee-invention-assignment-agreements); [NY Senate statute page](https://nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LAB/203-F); [Wilson Sonsini](https://wsgr.com/en/insights/new-york-redefines-the-permissible-scope-of-invention-assignment-provisions.html)
- NY 203-f only excludes inventions developed with employer "trade secrets," not other confidential/proprietary information — [search summary of firm alerts incl. Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/new-york-narrows-the-scope-of-employee-invention-assignment-provisions/)
- **New Jersey, N.J.S.A. 34:1B-265**: P.L. 2017, c.346, effective 2018; makes provisions covering such own-time inventions unenforceable; omits "directly"; silent on burden of proof — [Justia NJ 34:1B-265](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-34/section-34-1b-265/); [NJ P.L. 2017 c.346](https://pub.njleg.gov/bills/2016/pl17/346_.HTM); [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2018/07/invention-assignment-agreements-how-to-avoid-pitf/)
- **North Carolina, G.S. 66-57.1**: assignment provisions do not apply to inventions developed entirely on the employee's own time without employer equipment, supplies, facility or trade secret information, except those relating to the employer's business or actual/demonstrably anticipated R&D or resulting from work for the employer; employee bears the burden of proof; §66-57.2 bars requiring such a provision as a condition of employment — [NC G.S. 66-57.1](https://ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_66/GS_66-57.1.html); [NC Art. 10A](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/ByArticle/Chapter_66/Article_10A.html)
- **Delaware, 19 Del. C. §805** ("Employee's right to certain inventions"): described as nearly identical to the California model; employer may not require an unenforceable provision as a condition of employment — [Justia DE 19 §805](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/title-19/chapter-8/section-805); [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2018/07/invention-assignment-agreements-how-to-avoid-pitf/) (current operative text not retrieved)
- Illinois (765 ILCS 1060/2) and North Carolina place burden on the employee; Washington (RCW 49.44.140, 1979), Illinois, Kansas, Minnesota impose employer notice obligations — [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2018/07/invention-assignment-agreements-how-to-avoid-pitf/)
- Nevada's statute makes patentable inventions presumptively the employer's property (opposite direction) — [search summary of Foley / state law charts](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2018/07/invention-assignment-agreements-how-to-avoid-pitf/)
- 50-state chart (Thomson Reuters, dated 2014) and practitioner lists cover CA, DE, IL, KS, MN, NV, NJ, NC, UT, WA; NY added 2023 — [Thomson Reuters chart](https://legal.thomsonreuters.com/en/insights/documents/assignment-of-employee-inventions-state-laws-chart-I0755f93e5e3511e498db8b09b4f043e0); [Hinshaw](https://www.hinshawlaw.com/en/insights/blogs/employment-law-observer/new-york-limits-employee-invention-assignment-provisions)
- Search found no new state invention-assignment law enacted in 2025 or 2026 — [search results incl. Mondaq 2025 update](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/unitedstates/trade-secrets/1717630/employeeemployer-intellectual-property-disputes-understanding-ownership-and-recent-legal-developments-2025-update)

### Inferences
- East Coast statute states: NY, NJ, DE, NC. Non-statute East Coast states (FL, GA, PA, MA, VA, MD, CT, RI, NH, VT, ME, SC, DC): a broad assignment clause is enforced as written subject to general contract/public-policy limits; courts may still read "relates to business" clauses narrowly, but there is no statutory floor.
- Even in NY/NJ/DE/NC, the user's project likely falls within the "relates to employer's business" exception, so the statute's protection may not apply. Statutes are most useful for unrelated hobby projects.
- Choice-of-law clauses matter: a CA-headquartered employer's PIIA often includes a 2870 notice exhibit; protection under a given statute generally tracks where the employee works, but this was not verified per state.

### Gaps
- Current verbatim text of DE §805, NJ 34:1B-265, NY 203-f not retrieved (primary sites blocked); conditions summarized from secondary sources.
- Utah, Kansas, Minnesota, Illinois, Washington specifics not individually verified (not East Coast).
- Could not confirm whether any East Coast non-statute state (e.g., MA, PA) has pending invention-assignment bills in 2026.

## 3. Non-compete enforceability status (late 2026), East Coast states, and the FTC rule

### Takeaway
The FTC rule is dead: vacated nationally by N.D. Tex. (Aug. 2024); FTC voted 3-1 on Sept. 5, 2025 to drop its appeals and accede to vacatur; reportedly removed from the CFR in Feb. 2026. Non-competes are a state-law question. East Coast landscape ranges from employer-friendly Florida (CHOICE Act, up to 4-year covenants for high earners, eff. July 1, 2025) to restricted DC, MA, VA, MD. New York still has no general ban (2023 bill vetoed; 2025-26 bills stalled).

### Cited Findings
- **FTC**: Sept. 5, 2025, Commission voted 3-1 to dismiss appeals in Ryan LLC v. FTC (5th Cir.) and Properties of the Villages v. FTC (11th Cir.) and accede to vacatur; the N.D. Tex. Aug. 2024 nationwide set-aside stands; FTC shifting to case-by-case enforcement and issued an RFI on noncompetes — [FTC press release](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/federal-trade-commission-files-accede-vacatur-non-compete-clause-rule); [Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/ftc_abandons_appeals_decisions_striking_down_noncompete_rule_restrictive_covenants_remain_0925.html); [Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/ftc-shifts-approach-to-non-compete-enforcement)
- Rule formally removed from the federal code in February 2026 — [Vaquill blog roundup (secondary; verify)](https://www.vaquill.ai/blog/non-compete-q3-state-updates)
- **New York**: no statewide ban; Hochul vetoed S3100A in Dec. 2023; S4641 / S9759 / A10023 would ban most noncompetes except for earners >$500,000; bill stalled at end of 2026 session, may roll into 2027; narrower healthcare bill S10722 introduced (Oct. 2026 alert); NYC Intro 115 pending in committee — [Seyfarth 2026 session update (via search)](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/employment-updates-from-the-end-of-new-yorks-2026-legislative-session.html); [Holland & Knight, Oct. 26, 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/proposed-new-york-bill-targets-healthcare-non-competes); [Sullivan & Cromwell, Apr. 2026](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/blogs/2026/April/New-York-Senate-Re-Introduces-Bill-Prohibit-Certain-Non-Compete-Agreements); [intro.nyc 115](https://intro.nyc/0115-2026+). Conflicting listing: LegiScan shows S09228 (Feb. 2026) died in committee — [LegiScan](https://legiscan.com/NY/bill/S09228/)
- NY "Trapped at Work Act" (signed Dec. 19, 2025; amended Feb. 13, 2026, narrowed and delayed one year) bans employment promissory notes/stay-or-pay repayment provisions — [Eversheds Sutherland fall 2026 roundup](https://www.eversheds-sutherland.com/en/united-states/insights/state-noncompete-developments-fall-2026-2027-roundup); [Katz Banks Kumin, Mar. 2026](https://katzbanks.com/employment-law-blog/noncompete-agreements-whats-the-status-of-laws-restricting-them-nationwide-march-2026-update/)
- **Massachusetts** (Noncompetition Agreement Act, M.G.L. c.149 §24L, 2018): requires garden leave of at least 50% of highest annualized base salary in prior 2 years, pro rata over the restricted period, or "other mutually-agreed upon consideration"; unenforceable against FLSA non-exempt employees, those terminated without cause or laid off, interns/students, and those 18 or younger; not retroactive; max 12 months (statute) — [M.G.L. c.149 §24L](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXXI/Chapter149/Section24l); [Littler FAQ](https://www.littler.com/news-analysis/asap/frequently-asked-questions-about-new-massachusetts-noncompetition-agreement-act)
- **Virginia**: SB 1218 (signed Mar. 24, 2025, eff. July 1, 2025) extends ban to all FLSA non-exempt employees (previously "low-wage"); $10,000 civil penalty per violation; exempt employees above average weekly wage can still be bound — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/employment-law-watch/102lpcr/virginia-expands-non-compete-restrictions-to-cover-all-non-exempt-employees/); [Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/changes-to-virginias-noncompete-statute/). Further 2026 amendment (eff. July 1, 2026): noncompete unenforceable against employee terminated without cause unless employer pays severance/other monetary payment; prospective only — [Eversheds Sutherland roundup](https://www.eversheds-sutherland.com/en/united-states/insights/state-noncompete-developments-fall-2026-2027-roundup) (single-source; verify)
- **District of Columbia**: noncompetes allowed only for "highly compensated employees"; 2026 threshold $162,164; agreement must be provided ≥14 days before start/effect — [DC DOES 2026 notice](https://does.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/does/publication/attachments/2026%20Ban%20on%20Non-Compete%20Clauses.pdf); [Epstein Becker Green 2026 thresholds](https://www.ebglaw.com/trade-secrets-employee-mobility/raising-the-cost-of-noncompetes-2026-state-noncompete-salary-threshold-changes)
- **Maryland**: noncompetes void for employees earning ≤150% of state minimum wage (~$49,920 in 2026 at $16/hr); 2026 HB 1016 (Ch. 301) amends provisions — reported variously as relocation/reorganization voiding or architect-specific; unclear — [Foley 2026 overview](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/07/noncompete-agreements-federal-state-overview/); [MD HB1016 Ch. 301](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/Chapters_noln/CH_301_hb1016e.pdf)
- **Florida CHOICE Act** (HB 1219, became law ~July 3, 2025 without governor's signature, effective July 1, 2025): covered noncompetes and garden leave up to 4 years for "covered employees" (earning more than twice the annual mean wage of the county per firm summaries) whose primary place of work is in Florida, or with a Florida-HQ employer choosing Florida law; court must preliminarily enjoin unless employee proves by clear and convincing evidence otherwise; garden leave paid at base salary, no work required after first 90 days; non-qualifying agreements still governed by Fla. Stat. 542.335 (already employer-friendly) — [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/5/florida-choice-act-new-protections-for-noncompete-and-garden-leave-agreements); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/05/floridas-new-choice-act-authorizes-garden-leave-makes-4-year-noncompete-agreements-easier-to-enforce); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/enhanced-noncompetition-bill-becomes-law-in-florida); [FL bill text](https://flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2025/1219/BillText/c1/PDF)
- **Georgia**: Restrictive Covenants Act (O.C.G.A. 13-8-50 et seq.) permits reasonable noncompetes and lets courts modify ("blue pencil") overbroad covenants; §13-8-57 sets time presumptions — [Justia O.C.G.A. 13-8-57](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-13/chapter-8/article-4/section-13-8-57); [SHRM Atlanta, July 2026](https://www.shrmatlanta.org/2026/07/27/a-practical-guide-to-georgia-non-competes/)
- **Pennsylvania**: Fair Contracting for Health Care Practitioners Act (HB 1633) limits healthcare noncompetes only; other employees governed by common-law reasonableness — [Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/pennsylvania-limits-noncompete-agreements-for-health-care-practitioners.html)
- **Maine** LD 2200 (eff. July 29, 2026) bans healthcare-practitioner noncompetes; Maine notice rules require disclosure before offer and copy 3 business days before signing — [Eversheds Sutherland roundup](https://www.eversheds-sutherland.com/en/united-states/insights/state-noncompete-developments-fall-2026-2027-roundup)
- Non-East-Coast context: Washington ESHB 1155 (signed Mar. 2026) voids nearly all noncompetes eff. June 30, 2027 — [Eversheds Sutherland roundup](https://www.eversheds-sutherland.com/en/united-states/insights/state-noncompete-developments-fall-2026-2027-roundup)

### Inferences
- Non-competes chiefly bite *after* leaving; during employment, the duty of loyalty and moonlighting/conflict clauses control. A paid hosted version competing for SMB/MSP email-security customers is the scenario most likely to trigger both an in-term loyalty claim and a post-term noncompete claim.
- A software engineer at a cybersecurity vendor is typically FLSA-exempt and well-paid, so VA/MA/MD/DC low-wage carve-outs likely do not protect them; MA's garden-leave/consideration and termination-without-cause rules may.
- Choice-of-law/forum clauses (e.g., a CA-HQ employer selecting CA law; CA Bus. & Prof. Code 16600 et seq. voids noncompetes) could help an employee — not verified for this employer.

## 4. Trade secret risk (DTSA / state UTSA): employer detection logic, threat intel, customer info vs. general skill

### Takeaway
The biggest legal risk for a same-domain side project is misappropriation: reuse of employer detection rules/models, threat-intel feeds, internal datasets (including customer email samples), roadmaps, or customer/prospect lists. General skill, experience and publicly known techniques remain the employee's; specific confidential compilations do not. DTSA rejects "inevitable disclosure" as a basis for injunctions, but some states (e.g., PA) have applied it.

### Cited Findings
- DTSA (2016) explicitly rejects the inevitable disclosure doctrine under federal law (injunctions must be based on evidence of threatened misappropriation, not merely what the person knows), though state law still governs in state claims — [Fisher Phillips, DTSA and inevitable disclosure (via search)](https://www.fisherphillips.com/Non-Compete-and-Trade-Secrets/the-dtsa-and-inevitable-disclosure)
- Pennsylvania: Third Circuit applied a "substantial likelihood" of disclosure standard under PA law (inevitable-disclosure-like injunctions possible) — [Buchanan Ingersoll & Rooney](https://bipc.com/the-inevitable-disclosure-doctrine-in-pennsylvania-prohibiting-former-employees-from-working-for-a-competitor)
- Georgia: standalone inevitable-disclosure claim not cognizable; a former employee's memorized customer knowledge was held not a trade secret under the GTSA while tangible customer lists were protectable — [Chambers Trade Secrets 2026, Georgia](https://chambers.com/practice-guides/guides/trade-secrets-2026/usa-georgia/trends-and-developments)
- Core difficulty: distinguishing protectable trade secrets from non-protectable knowledge and skill retained in a former employee's memory — [FindLaw corporate, inevitable disclosure doctrine](https://corporate.findlaw.com/intellectual-property/the-inevitable-disclosure-doctrine-a-necessary-and-precise-tool.html)
- NY 203-f's carve-out is lost if the invention used employer "trade secret information" — [Cozen O'Connor](https://www.cozen.com/news-resources/publications/2023/new-law-in-ny-prohibits-certain-employee-invention-assignment-agreements)

### Inferences
- For an email-security engineer, the high-risk categories are: proprietary detection heuristics/ML features and thresholds, labeled phishing corpora derived from customer mail, internal threat-intel/IOC feeds, evasion findings, pricing, MSP partner lists, and roadmap. Using any of these (even from memory, in a way that tracks specific proprietary details) creates DTSA/UTSA exposure and can independently strip statutory invention carve-outs.
- Lower-risk: public research, open datasets, open-source models, public RFCs (SPF/DKIM/DMARC), general ML/LLM engineering skill.
- Contract confidentiality clauses typically define confidential info more broadly than "trade secret" and survive termination indefinitely; breach of contract may be easier to prove than misappropriation.
- Clean-room hygiene (personal hardware, personal accounts, no employer data, dated commit history, public-only sources documented) is evidence, not immunity.

### Gaps
- No DTSA statutory text retrieved (18 U.S.C. 1836 et seq.); whistleblower immunity notice (18 U.S.C. 1833(b)) not verified here.
- No case law found specifically on security-vendor employees' side projects.

## 5. Moonlighting / off-duty conduct protections and their limits for competitive conflicts

### Takeaway
A few states protect lawful off-duty activity (NY Labor Law 201-d is the main East Coast example), but these laws exempt activity creating a material conflict with the employer's trade secrets, proprietary information or business interests, and the "recreational" category covers uncompensated leisure. A competing, monetized security product would very likely fall outside protection; most other East Coast states offer no general moonlighting protection (at-will employment + contract terms control).

### Cited Findings
- NY Labor Law 201-d bars adverse action for off-duty political activity, legal recreational activity, legal use of consumable products (incl. cannabis after MRTA 2021), outside working hours, off premises and without employer equipment/property — [NY Senate 201-d](https://nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LAB/201-D); [Mintz on MRTA amendment](https://mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2226/2021-05-03-new-york-amends-its-duty-conduct-law-account-marijuana)
- "Recreational activities" are defined as lawful leisure-time activities "for which the employee receives no compensation" (e.g., sports, games, hobbies, exercise, reading) — [Justia NY 201-d](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-york/2013/lab/article-7/201-d/)
- Protection does not apply to activity that "creates a material conflict of interest related to the employer's trade secrets, proprietary information or other proprietary or business interest" — [NY Senate 201-d](https://nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LAB/201-D); application described by NYSBA as unsettled — [NYSBA](https://nysba.org/can-private-employees-be-fired-for-out-of-office-political-speech/)
- Enforcement: AG, plus private action for equitable relief and damages — [NY Senate 201-d](https://nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LAB/201-D)

### Inferences
- A paid hosted phishing-triage service is compensated (not "recreational") and in the employer's market (likely "material conflict"), so 201-d likely offers no protection. A purely unpaid OSS project is arguably a hobby but still faces the conflict exception.
- Separate from statutes, the common-law duty of loyalty during employment generally bars competing with the current employer or soliciting its customers; preparing to compete is often permitted, actually competing is not (general principle; not sourced here).
- PIIAs commonly require disclosure/approval of outside business activities in the same field; a written approval is the cleanest path.

### Gaps
- Did not research other East Coast off-duty statutes (e.g., CT, NJ lawful-activity/tobacco statutes; NC lawful products law) — believed narrow, unverified.
- Common-law duty of loyalty case law per East Coast state not sourced.
- Non-solicit enforceability (customers/employees) per state not specifically researched; MA §24L excludes non-solicits from its restrictions (common-law governs).
