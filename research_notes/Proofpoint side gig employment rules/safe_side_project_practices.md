# Safe Side-Project Practices for Cybersecurity-Vendor Employees (applied to a Proofpoint-type support SME building an open-source AI phishing-triage CLI)

Scope note: these are research notes, not legal advice. US focus, compiled 2026-11-22. Several primary hosts could not be reached from this research session: opensource.google, proofpoint.com (direct fetch), lobste.rs and mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu. Their content comes only from search-result snippets, and the claims that rest on them are marked. Forum sources (Blind, HN) are anecdotal and labelled as such.

## 1. How big tech/security companies handle employee open-source and side-project approval

### Takeaway
Policies sit on a spectrum. At one end is GitHub's BEIPA: the employee owns free-time work, and the company only gets a non-exclusive license to work related to its business. At the other is Google's IARC: the company owns IP broadly and the employee must ask for a copyright waiver, which can take months and may be refused. Most employers fall in between. Typical rules are no company time, resources or data, no conflict of interest, and prior written approval for any outside business. The actual contract and the employee's state law decide the outcome.

### Cited Findings
- **GitHub BEIPA (CC0-licensed, reusable):** the company gets "exclusive control of IP created in the scope of an employee's job." The employee gets exclusive control of IP created outside the job and unrelated to the business. IP created outside the job but *related* to the business belongs to the employee, and the company gets a "non-exclusive and unlimited license." Employees may contribute to open source without permission and may own closed-source side projects. BEIPA says it is not legal advice. Version 2.0 adds German-law language. — [GitHub BEIPA repo](https://github.com/github/balanced-employee-ip-agreement)
- In 2017 GitHub publicly said its staff keep the IP in personal projects even when they use company resources. — [Quartz](https://qz.com/937038/github-now-lets-its-workers-keep-the-ip-when-they-use-company-resources-for-personal-projects)
- **Google IARC (Invention Assignment Review Committee):** according to Google's open-source docs as quoted in search snippets, full-time employees in good standing may ask Google to waive its copyright through IARC. Authoring a project "entirely independently from Google" requires permission via go/iarc. — [opensource.google "Creating"](https://opensource.google/docs/creating/) and [Getting Approval](https://opensource.google/docs/releasing/approval/) (pages not directly fetchable here; content from snippets)
- Google's agreement lets it claim IP in code employees write for outside projects. In a 2019 case, one engineer's review took about a year, and he stopped maintaining his project in the meantime. — [Business Insider India](https://www.businessinsider.in/tech/enterprise/news/google-can-claim-rights-over-code-that-its-employees-write-for-outside-projects-and-former-googlers-say-this-policy-could-potentially-put-other-projects-at-risk/articleshow/74031034.cms)
- A 2019 IARC request was rejected, and the engineer was told to follow his employment agreement. Google's claim reaches "projects personally performed on holidays." — [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20191220-google-employee-iarc-intellectual-property); HN discussion: [HN 21823413](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21823413) (anecdotal)
- (Anecdotal) Googlers report that any change of scope or even a project rename requires a fresh IARC approval. — [Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/googles-bureaucracy-approval-process-of-personal-open-source-contributions-and-paper-publications-e6nwnnjg)
- **Microsoft:** there is no single central approval body. Open-source guidance lives on an internal intranet and is vetted by Open Source Champions and an Executive Council. Each org has a dedicated open-source attorney. — [Linux Foundation blog](https://linuxfoundation.org/blog/open-source-program-microsoft-open-source-thrives); [TODO Group case study](https://todogroup.org/guides/casestudies/microsoft)
- (Anecdotal) Microsoft employees report that any side business, even an LLC, needs approval, especially if it is tech-related. Reported baseline rules: no work on Microsoft time, no conflict of interest, no company resources. — [Blind: moonlighting at Microsoft](https://www.teamblind.com/post/moonlighting-at-microsoft-tkkvwtg8); [Blind: part-time entrepreneurship](https://www.teamblind.com/post/part-time-entrepreneurship-while-employed-at-microsoft-xk63cpj2)
- (Anecdotal) Apple reportedly wants employees to check in before any side hustle. — [Blind: Apple side projects](https://www.teamblind.com/post/can-apple-employees-publish-their-side-projects-as-open-source-on-github-hh2qwze8)
- Comparable codes: Salesforce requires approval before outside business activities begin, and Sophos (a security vendor) requires written approval where dual employment could create a perceived conflict. — [Salesforce Code of Conduct](https://www.salesforce.com/company/legal/compliance/code-of-conduct/building-trust-company/); [Sophos Code of Conduct](https://www.sophos.com/pt-br/legal/code-of-conduct)
- **Proofpoint:** employee standards are set out in its "Business Conduct Guidelines," with periodic training. The text was not retrievable here. — [Proofpoint Modern Slavery statement (PDF)](https://www.proofpoint.com/sites/default/files/legal-documents/pfpt-en-au-modern-slavery-act-and-human-trafficking-statement.pdf); [Proofpoint governance page](https://www.proofpoint.com/us/legal/governance); [Business Ethics](https://www.proofpoint.com/us/legal/corporate-responsibility/business-ethics)
- Proofpoint also has a published antitrust compliance policy, which may bear on dealings with competitors. — [Proofpoint antitrust policy PDF](https://www.proofpoint.com/sites/default/files/legal-documents/antitrust-compliance-policy.pdf)

### Inferences
- A typical approval request contains: project name and one-paragraph description; whether it is open source and under which license; whether it will be commercial (hosted SaaS or consulting); target users and market; how it relates or does not relate to the employer's products and customers; confirmation of personal time, personal devices and accounts, and no employer code, data, samples, detection logic or confidential information; a list of third-party dependencies and APIs (here, LLM providers, Microsoft Graph and Gmail APIs); a promise to re-disclose if scope changes; and a request for a written IP waiver or acknowledgment. This is synthesized from the Google, Microsoft and BEIPA patterns above, not taken from any published form.
- Because Google treats renames and scope changes as new requests, the safest request describes the full intended scope up front, including possible hosted or consulting monetization. An approval for "a free CLI" may not cover a later SaaS.
- Cisco and Palo Alto Networks policies: I found no public primary source. See Gaps.

### Gaps
- No public text of Proofpoint's Business Conduct Guidelines, its outside-activity or moonlighting rule, or its invention assignment agreement. The user should read their own signed PIIA/CIIAA and the guidelines on the intranet.
- No primary source found for Cisco's or Palo Alto Networks' employee side-project or open-source approval processes.
- I could not fetch Google's current (2026) IARC page directly. The process details come from 2019–2020 reporting and search snippets.

## 2. Real cases where side projects were claimed or led to termination or lawsuits

### Takeaway
The worst outcomes share a few features. The contract assigned "inventions" broadly, the employee asked to be released only after the idea was already related to the job, or the employee carried employer files out the door. Disputes are expensive even when the employee partly wins (Bratz). The one email-security case found, Proofpoint v. Vade, is a trade-secret case about a former executive taking knowledge to a competitor. It shows Proofpoint litigates aggressively over email-filtering IP.

### Cited Findings
- **Mattel v. MGA (Bratz), 9th Cir. 2010, 616 F.3d 904:** designer Carter Bryant's agreement assigned "inventions" conceived "at any time during my employment." The Ninth Circuit held that phrase ambiguous (it could mean only work hours). The district court erred in telling the jury, without extrinsic evidence, that "ideas" were assigned, and the broad constructive trust over the Bratz brand was vacated and remanded. — [Ninth Circuit opinion PDF](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2010/07/28/09-55673.pdf); [Proskauer summary](https://calemploymentlawupdate.proskauer.com/2010/09/employees-ideas-may-not-have-been-the-property-of-his-former-employer/); [WIPO Magazine](https://www.wipo.int/wipo_magazine/en/2011/04/article_0006.html)
- Commentators drew a drafting lesson: employers revised assignment clauses to name "ideas" explicitly. Modern agreements are therefore likely broader than Bryant's was. — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/ninth-circuit-finds-employment-agreement-ambiguous-to-whether-employee-s-ideas-were-assigned); [Adams Drafting](https://www.adamsdrafting.com/a-defined-term-lesson-from-the-bratz-doll-saga/)
- **Alcatel (DSC) v. Evan Brown, Texas, ruling around 2002:** Brown asked DSC to release a solution idea (converting legacy code) so he could pursue it himself. He came back from vacation, was fired and was served with a suit claiming DSC owned his idea. The Texas court held his 1987 agreement, which required disclosure of job-related inventions, enforceable, so even undocumented "thoughts" counted as an invention to disclose. He was ordered to pay about $332,000 in costs and eventually had to disclose the idea. — [Pinsent Masons Out-Law](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/ex-employee8217s-idea-is-owned-by-alcatel); [The Register](https://www.theregister.co.uk/content/7/26627.html); [D Magazine "Who owns your brain"](https://www.dmagazine.com/publications/d-magazine/2001/june/street-talk-who-owns-your-brain/)
- Secondary commentary describes Brown as a seven-year fight that ended with him writing out the code at the company's offices. — [Wharton Mack Institute paper (snippet only)](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Ren-Charlotte_The-Legal-Environment-of-Side-Project-Ownership-and-It-Innovation.pdf). That paper also says Texas gives employers more contractual power over side projects than restrictive-statute states do (snippet only).
- **Waymo/Google v. Levandowski and Uber:** Levandowski downloaded about 14,000 "Project Chauffeur" files to a personal laptop in December 2015, left in 2016, and founded Otto, which Uber bought for roughly $600–680M (sources differ). Uber settled the civil case by giving Waymo about $245M in equity. Levandowski pleaded guilty to one count of trade-secret theft and in August 2020 was sentenced to 18 months, a $95,000 fine and $756,499.22 in restitution. The judge rejected the argument that no use of the secrets had been proven. — [DOJ press release](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ndca/pr/former-uber-executive-sentenced-18-months-jail-trade-secret-theft-google); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/08/04/anthony-levandowski-sentenced-to-18-months-in-prison-as-new-4b-lawsuit-against-uber-is-filed); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/news/anthony-levandowski-google-engineer-uber-prison-stealing-robocar-secrets)
- **Email-security example: Proofpoint/Cloudmark v. Vade Secure and Olivier Lemarié (N.D. Cal., verdict August 2021):** a jury found that Vade, an email-security competitor, misappropriated 15 of 20 asserted trade secrets "willfully and maliciously." The trade secrets belonged to Cloudmark, which Proofpoint had acquired, and Lemarié was a former Cloudmark executive. The jury also found copyright infringement and that Lemarié breached his employee agreement. Damages were about $13.4M in unjust enrichment plus $480K for breach of contract. — [The Register](https://theregister.com/2021/08/24/proofpoint_wins_14m_vade/); [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/vade-secure-ordered-pay-14-million-proofpoint-ip-theft-lawsuit); [Channel Futures](https://channelfutures.com/regulation-compliance/jury-vade-secure-willfully-and-maliciously-misappropriated-proofpoint-trade-secrets); [Proofpoint blog](https://www.proofpoint.com/us/blog/corporate-news/jury-unanimously-finds-vade-secure-willfully-misappropriated-proofpoint-trade)
- In later rulings the court was skeptical of doubled exemplary damages (October 2021) and denied Proofpoint's attorney-fee request (September 2023). This rests on Law360 only. — [Law360 case page](https://law360.com/insurance-authority/cases/5d383734f3f3b01747f3f61f/articles)
- **Match/Tinder v. Bumble (2018):** the suit included allegations that former Tinder employees who moved to Bumble used design ideas and confidential information. Bumble countersued. — [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2018/03/27/match-group-sues-bumble-former-tinder-employees-misappropriated-trade-secrets/id=95003/); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2018/10/30/660006488)
- (Anecdotal) A 2020-era report says Bumble claimed IP rights over an employee's open-source code. I could not verify details because the source host was unreachable. — [lobste.rs thread](https://lobste.rs/s/rsbfg1/bumble_claims_ip_rights_on_employee_s_open)
- (Anecdotal) In "My company wants my side project. What can I do?" commenters note that putting an open-source license on code you don't own gives you nothing. The Nginx dispute in Russia, where Rambler claimed Igor Sysoev's code, is often cited as a case of a company claiming open source written on the developer's own time. — [HN 21786111](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21786111)
- **AI-assistant angle (2025–26):** one commentary describes an employer claiming a developer's personal app built with an AI coding assistant. The argument was that the assistant had company files open in the IDE, so its output was derivative of company IP. — [Legal Layer Substack "Who Owns the Code Claude Wrote?"](https://legallayer.substack.com/p/who-owns-the-claude-code-wrote) (single commentary; treat as illustrative)
- **Employee-status test (UK, Redrock v Hinkley):** ownership turned on whether each program was made "in pursuance of" employment. Time and place alone were not decisive. — [Artificer Legal](https://artificer.legal/employee-code-copyright-ownership) (UK law, for analogy only)
- **Burden of proof (California):** in Venture Corp. v. Barrett (N.D. Cal.), the employee bore the burden of proving that inventions fell within the Labor Code §2870 exemption, and the question went to a jury. — [Justia docket 278](https://docs.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/5:2013cv03384/268370/278)
- **Post-employment assignment (Whitewater West v. Alleshouse, Fed. Cir. 2020):** a clause assigning inventions conceived *after* employment was held void under Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §16600. — [Federal Circuit opinion](https://www.cafc.uscourts.gov/opinions-orders/19-1852.opinion.11-19-2020_1688201.pdf); [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2020/11/23/federal-circuit-reverses-district-court-sides-inventors-analysis-california-employment-contract-law/id=127492/)

### Inferences
- Lessons:
  1. Asking for release *after* the idea overlaps your job invites a firing and a lawsuit (Brown).
  2. Copying even one employer file to personal devices turns an IP dispute into a trade-secret or criminal matter (Levandowski, Vade).
  3. Courts read contract wording closely, and post-Bratz contracts are drafted broadly.
  4. Under §2870-style statutes the employee carries the burden of proof, so contemporaneous records matter.
- The Vade case is the most relevant warning. Proofpoint has shown it will litigate trade-secret and breach-of-agreement claims in the email-filtering space through a jury verdict. A side project that looks like it draws on Proofpoint detection know-how would land in exactly that frame.

### Gaps
- I found no documented case of a Proofpoint, Mimecast, Abnormal or other email-security *employee* being fired or sued over a personal open-source tool.
- I found no documented case involving a security researcher's personal research tool being claimed by an employer in the US (searched indirectly; nothing reliable surfaced).
- Bumble open-source claim details are unverified.

## 3. Practical rules: separation, no employer data, timeline, disclosure, approval, non-competing angle, licensing

### Takeaway
Two things drive outcomes: the signed agreement and state law. Roughly 11 states, including New York (since 2023), New Jersey, Illinois, Delaware, Washington and North Carolina, void assignment of inventions made on the employee's own time without employer equipment or trade secrets. Every one of these statutes carves out inventions that *relate to the employer's business* or result from the employee's work. A phishing-triage tool built by an email-security employee almost certainly "relates to the employer's business," so the statutory safe harbor probably does not protect it. Written approval or a waiver is therefore the main protection, and separation hygiene is necessary but not enough.

### Cited Findings
- **California Labor Code §2870:** this is the template statute. It excludes from assignment inventions developed entirely on the employee's own time, without the employer's equipment, supplies, facilities or trade secrets. The exception does not apply to inventions that relate to the employer's business, or its actual or demonstrably anticipated R&D, or that result from work performed for the employer. §2872 requires employers to give written notice of this limit. — [Cal. Lab. Code §2870 (Justia)](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/2020/code-lab/division-3/chapter-2/article-3-5/section-2870/); [§2872](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/2014/code-lab/division-3/chapter-2/article-3.5/section-2872/)
- **New York Labor Law §203-f:** signed September 15, 2023, effective immediately. It voids assignment of own-time, own-equipment inventions *except* those that relate to the employer's business or anticipated R&D, or result from the employee's work for the employer. — [NY Senate §203-F](https://nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LAB/203-F); [Wilson Sonsini](https://wsgr.com/en/insights/new-york-redefines-the-permissible-scope-of-invention-assignment-provisions.html); [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2023/10/employers-beware-changes-to-new-york-invention)
- With New York, about 11 states limit invention assignment: CA, DE (19 Del. C. §805), IL (765 ILCS 1060/2), KS, MN, NV, NJ, NC, UT, WA (the earliest, 1979) and NY. KS and MN add notice requirements. — [Troutman Pepper](https://www.troutman.com/insights/new-york-narrows-the-scope-of-employee-invention-assignment-provisions/); [Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/new-york-governor-signs-law-limiting-employment-clauses-for-assignment-of-employee-inventions); [Thomson Reuters state chart](https://legal.thomsonreuters.com/en/insights/documents/assignment-of-employee-inventions-state-laws-chart-I0755f93e5e3511e498db8b09b4f043e0) (chart may be dated)
- Typical clauses assign inventions made during employment "regardless of time, place, or ownership of equipment" if they relate to the business. States without a statute (for example Texas) enforce such clauses more readily. — [Wharton Mack Institute paper (snippet)](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Ren-Charlotte_The-Legal-Environment-of-Side-Project-Ownership-and-It-Innovation.pdf); Alcatel v. Brown above
- Even under BEIPA, the most employee-friendly model, IP related to the business carries a company license. An employee cannot make related work fully "clean." — [GitHub BEIPA](https://github.com/github/balanced-employee-ip-agreement)
- Using AI coding tools on a machine that has employer files open created a derivative-work argument in at least one reported dispute. — [Legal Layer Substack](https://legallayer.substack.com/p/who-owns-the-claude-code-wrote)

### Inferences (practical checklist)
1. **Read the actual documents first:** the PIIA/CIIAA (look for "relates to the business," "ideas," "at any time," and any prior-inventions schedule), the Business Conduct Guidelines (outside activities, conflicts, publishing, social media), and any moonlighting policy. Note which state law governs: the user's residence state, possibly the contract's choice-of-law clause, and Proofpoint's California base, which is unverified here.
2. **Get written approval before writing code,** or at minimum before publishing. Ask Legal/Ethics, not only your manager, for an IP waiver or an acknowledgment that the project is employee-owned. Describe the full future scope (open-source CLI, possible hosted version, possible consulting) so a later expansion does not need a fresh request, or so it is clear that it will.
3. **Separation:** a personal laptop never enrolled in employer MDM; personal GitHub, email, cloud and LLM API accounts paid personally; no employer VPN or Wi-Fi; work only outside business hours. For a US Eastern shift worker, keep commit timestamps outside scheduled shift and on-call windows, because git timestamps are evidence.
4. **No employer inputs:** no customer emails or samples, TAP/TRAP logs, threat intel feeds, internal detection rules, YARA or signature content, support-ticket data, internal runbooks, roadmap, or pricing. Use only public corpora and synthetic or self-generated test mail. Do not let an AI assistant see employer files: separate machine, separate account.
5. **Document provenance:** dated design notes, a public repo history from day one, a README stating "independent personal project, not affiliated with [employer], built from public sources," and a list of the public datasets, RFCs and docs relied on. This addresses the burden-of-proof problem seen in Venture v. Barrett.
6. **Prior inventions:** if any relevant code or idea predates employment, it belongs on the prior-inventions schedule. Adding it later is weaker evidence.
7. **Licensing:** a permissive license (Apache-2.0 includes an explicit patent grant; MIT is simpler) suits a community SMB/MSP tool. AGPL deters competitors from offering a hosted clone, but it signals a commercial play. A license does not cure an ownership defect (HN commenters above): if the employer owns the code, the license is invalid.
8. **Non-competing angle:** build triage that runs natively on Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace APIs, does not integrate with or replicate Proofpoint products, and does not name or benchmark against the employer. Avoid marketing to MSPs that resell Proofpoint.
9. **Public persona:** do not cite the employer title in marketing for the project, and do not post about employer products or customers.

### Gaps
- No public sample approval form from any security vendor was found.
- I could not confirm which state's law governs a remote Eastern-time Proofpoint employee. That depends on the contract and residence and needs a lawyer.

## 4. Risk ranking: competing SMB/MSP product vs. free OSS vs. content vs. portfolio, and lower-risk niches

### Takeaway
Proofpoint sells directly into the SMB/MSP email-security channel. Essentials, rebranded as "Proofpoint 365 Total Protection," is an MSP-focused Microsoft 365 security suite with multi-tenant management and monthly billing, and it includes automatic post-delivery remediation and user phishing reporting (PhishAlarm). A paid hosted phishing-triage service for MSPs would therefore compete head-on with the employer, which is the highest-risk option. A free OSS CLI is lower risk but still "relates to the business." Content, teaching and adjacent non-email niches are lowest.

### Cited Findings
- Proofpoint says Essentials "is now Proofpoint 365 Total Protection: an all-in-one Microsoft 365 security suite built for MSPs." — [Proofpoint MSP page (snippet)](https://proofpoint.com/us/partners/msp); [Proofpoint SMB channel page](https://www.proofpoint.com/us/channel-partners-smb)
- The MSP program has Bronze through Platinum tiers, monthly utility billing, a multi-tenant console for downstream customers and, per a distributor, no minimum license quantity. — [Proofpoint MSP page](https://proofpoint.com/us/partners/msp); [Exclusive Networks](https://www.exclusive-networks.com/ch-fr/proofpoint-essentials-mssp-e-mail-sicherheit); [MSP solution brief PDF](https://www.proofpoint.com/sites/default/files/solution-briefs/pfpt-us-sb-msp-essentials.pdf)
- Essentials release notes describe automatic post-delivery retraction. It was initially malware-only, and in August 2025 TOAD and Impostor classifications were added as automatic-remediation triggers. — [Essentials Release Notes](https://help.proofpoint.com/Essentials/Additional_Resources/Release_Notes)
- PhishAlarm (the report button) and CLEAR (Closed-Loop Email Analysis and Response) automate the flow from user report through analysis to remediation. TRAP quarantines delivered malicious mail, including forwarded copies. — [PhishAlarm](https://www.proofpoint.com/au/products/security-awareness-training/phishalarm-email-reporting); [TRAP](https://www.proofpoint.com/au/products/email-protection/threat-response-auto-pull); [CLEAR blog](https://www.proofpoint.com/us/corporate-blog/post/new-clear-solution-automation-email-reporting-through-remediation); [Dark Reading on CLEAR](https://www.darkreading.com/cyber-risk/proofpoint-launches-closed-loop-email-analysis-and-response-solution-to-automate-end-user-reported-phishing-remediation)
- Proofpoint also sells a Managed Abuse Mailbox service, which is human-assisted triage of reported mail. — [Managed Abuse Mailbox](https://www.proofpoint.com/uk/products/premium-services/managed-abuse-mailbox)

### Inferences (risk ranking, highest to lowest; my synthesis, not from a source)
1. **Highest: paid hosted AI triage SaaS for SMBs/MSPs.** It overlaps directly with 365 Total Protection auto-remediation, PhishAlarm/CLEAR and Managed Abuse Mailbox, in the same channel. This is a likely conflict-of-interest violation, a "relates to business" IP claim under any state statute, and the profile Proofpoint has litigated before (Vade). Realistically it would require leaving the company, waiting out any non-solicit period, and building from scratch with no carried-over materials.
2. **High: paid consulting for SMBs/MSPs on email-security triage.** This competes for services revenue and partners, and it risks using customer knowledge. It would also probably need approval under typical outside-business rules.
3. **Medium-high: free OSS phishing-triage CLI for M365/Google Workspace.** There is no revenue conflict, but it still "relates to the business," so the employer could claim ownership or a license, and it could be seen as a free substitute for a paid feature. It is acceptable only with a written waiver or approval.
4. **Medium: job-portfolio project kept private or unpublished.** The ownership question is the same, but there is less exposure. It loses value if it cannot be shown publicly.
5. **Lower: content and teaching** (blog posts, YouTube, workshops on general phishing analysis, header forensics, DMARC/SPF/DKIM, LLM prompting for SOC analysts, built from public examples). Copyrighted content is usually less exposed under invention-assignment clauses, but publishing, social-media and confidentiality rules still apply. Check whether the policy requires pre-approval for public speaking about security.
6. **Lower-risk adjacent niches that still use the user's skills:**
   - (a) Email-authentication and deliverability tooling for nonprofits and small orgs (DMARC report parsing, SPF flattening checker). This is near the employer's business but often not a product line; verify.
   - (b) An LLM-agnostic support-engineering or ticket-triage tool, not security-specific.
   - (c) Generic SOC or IR training labs and CTF content using public datasets.
   - (d) Security-awareness content for a vertical such as churches or K-12, which is also lower revenue.
   - (e) Contributions to existing neutral OSS projects (for example, header parsers) rather than a branded competing tool.

   Every niche that touches phishing or email still needs a check against the agreement's "relates to business" wording. Proofpoint's portfolio is broad (email, awareness training, DLP, archiving), so few email-adjacent niches are truly outside it.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch proofpoint.com directly to confirm the current 365 Total Protection feature matrix by tier, or whether CLEAR, PhishAlarm or AI triage features are bundled for MSPs.
- No quantitative data exists on how often employers enforce against free OSS versus commercial side projects.

## 5. Conflicts of interest specific to support and threat roles

### Takeaway
A technical-support SME for TAP/TRAP/threat response holds the kind of knowledge trade-secret law protects: customer environments and pain points, detection and remediation behavior, internal tooling, known product gaps, and roadmap. A phishing-triage tool for the same customer segment is very hard to separate convincingly from that knowledge. The risk is less about copying code and more about "negative know-how," customer solicitation and the appearance of misusing confidential information.

### Cited Findings
- In Proofpoint v. Vade, the jury found trade-secret misappropriation and *breach of the employee agreement* by a former executive of the acquired company's email-filtering team. Know-how carried in a person's head, not only files, was at issue. — [The Register](https://theregister.com/2021/08/24/proofpoint_wins_14m_vade/); [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/vade-secure-ordered-pay-14-million-proofpoint-ip-theft-lawsuit)
- In Levandowski, the court rejected "no evidence of use" as mitigation. Taking the material was enough. — [DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ndca/pr/former-uber-executive-sentenced-18-months-jail-trade-secret-theft-google); [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/news/anthony-levandowski-google-engineer-uber-prison-stealing-robocar-secrets)

### Inferences
- Specific conflict vectors for this role:
  1. **Customer knowledge:** support staff see which SMBs and MSPs struggle with false positives, TRAP setup or abuse-mailbox volume. Targeting those MSPs for a side product looks like solicitation using confidential information.
  2. **Detection and remediation logic:** knowing how TAP scores or classifies threats, TRAP's matching behavior, and why certain lures evade detection. Encoding that, even from memory, in a triage heuristic is the Vade or Brown fact pattern.
  3. **Product gaps and roadmap:** building exactly what customers have asked for in support tickets or feature requests ("demonstrably anticipated R&D") is the clearest "relates to business" trigger.
  4. **Internal tooling:** recreating internal support scripts or triage playbooks.
  5. **Threat samples:** customer-submitted phish and malware are customer data under contract, so never use them as test or training data.
  6. **Duty of loyalty and time:** working on the project during shifts or on-call, or recommending one's own tool to customers on support calls, is a direct conflict.
- Mitigations: build only from public sources (RFCs, Microsoft Graph and Gmail API docs, public phishing datasets, public threat-intel blogs), keep a provenance log, make no claims about Proofpoint detection efficacy, never contact Proofpoint customers or partners about the project, and recuse from any customer conversation where the tool could come up.
- If the user plans to sell eventually, the cleanest path may be to leave Proofpoint first, honor any non-solicit or confidentiality terms, and build from a clean room. Some states also limit non-competes, for example California §16600 per Whitewater.
